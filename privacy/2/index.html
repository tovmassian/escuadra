Privacy policy

Last updated: 26 October 2026

This policy applies to Escuadra 2.0 and later. Privacy policy for earlier versions

Escuadra has no account, no sign-in and no advertising, and it never asks for your name, email or any contact details.

Your scores and settings are saved on your device. The only things that leave it are the ones described below, and none of them is linked to who you are.

Player photos

Escuadra does not ship player photos. They are loaded from Wikimedia Commons, run by the Wikimedia Foundation: a round's photos when the round starts, and a squad's photos on the Study screen as you scroll. Like any website, Wikimedia receives your device's IP address and the details of each request, including which photos were asked for, and so which players are in your round or squad. Escuadra sends nothing else to Wikimedia, and Wikimedia's own privacy policy applies to what it receives.

Photos already loaded may be kept in the app's cache on your device so that they appear faster. Without a connection the quiz works as usual and shows the team's colours and the shirt number in place of any photo not already in that cache.

Tapping a photo credit opens the photo's Wikimedia Commons page, or its licence on the Creative Commons website, in an in-app browser. Those sites have their own privacy policies, and Escuadra cannot see what you do there.

Sharing

Sharing a result creates a picture or a short text on your device and passes it to the app you pick in your phone's share menu. Escuadra does not see, send or keep what you share.

Anonymous usage statistics

Escuadra sends anonymous usage statistics to TelemetryDeck, an analytics service, unless you switch "Usage statistics" off in the app's Settings. Each one carries a hashed form of a random identifier created for these statistics (separate from the one the app-update check uses), a random session code, the time the statistic was made, the app's version and the update it is running (and on iOS its build number), whether your phone runs iOS or Android and the main version of its system, and the app's language. The session code is new each time the app starts or comes back after five minutes or more in the background, and when you switch "Usage statistics" back on. Like any online service, TelemetryDeck also receives your device's IP address and the standard technical details of each request, which on iOS include the app's build number and your device's full system version. TelemetryDeck's own privacy policy applies to what it receives. The identifier and the session code are random, not derived from your device, and not linked to you or your accounts. Neither Escuadra nor the developer shares them or the statistics with anyone else.

Without a connection, statistics wait on your device and are sent once there is one. Only the newest 500 are kept, and any older than 30 days are deleted unsent. Switching "Usage statistics" off deletes the ones waiting, and none are made while it is off. The statistic for Reset Escuadra is sent straight away, before the reset, so without a connection it is lost.

The statistics are: a session started; the app was started, with whether its language follows your phone's or was chosen in the app, your appearance setting, whether vibration is on, whether your phone is set to reduce motion, roughly how large your text is, and roughly how many teams you have starred and how many you have played; a team's screen was opened, with the team, whether it is a club or a nation, whether you came from Continue, Your squads or the team list, and whether you had searched; a round started, with the team, whether it is a club or a nation, the difficulty level, and whether it began from the team's screen, or, after a round, from Retry this round or Play level 2 or 3; a round finished, with the team, whether it is a club or a nation, the level, the score, whether it was a la escuadra, your best score for that team and level before it, how many questions were missed on the name, the position and the club or nationality, and roughly how long it took; a round was left before its end, with the team, whether it is a club or a nation, the level, the question reached, the score so far and roughly how long it took; the Study screen was opened, with the team, whether it is a club or a nation, where you opened it from and whether it shows cards or a list; Hide names was switched on, with the team and whether it is a club or a nation; a screen's How to play tips were closed, with which screen and whether you skipped them; the opening animation was tapped through; a result was passed to your phone's share menu, with the team, whether it is a club or a nation, the level, the score and whether it was a picture or text; a search in the team list found nothing, with whether it was among clubs or nations; and scores were reset, with whether it was one team (and which), all scores or everything. They name a team only by the app's own code for it, and never include player names, your answers or anything you type.

App updates

When Escuadra starts, it checks whether a newer version of the app is available from Expo, the service that delivers its updates. The check sends what is needed to deliver the right update: your device's operating system, the app's version and project, and a random identifier created for this installation. Escuadra does not link the identifier to you or to anything you do in the app. If the app crashed or failed to load an update while starting up, the next check also sends Expo the technical error message from that failure. Like any online service, Expo also receives your device's IP address with each check. Without a connection, the app skips the check and works as usual.

What is stored on your device

Escuadra saves twelve things locally, on your device: your best score for each team and difficulty level, from which it works out which levels you have completed; the last team and level you played; the teams you have starred; your appearance preference (Match phone, Light or Dark); your language choice; whether vibration is on; whether the Study screen shows cards or a list; which screens have shown you their How to play tips, and whether you skipped them; which app version last asked your phone to show a rating prompt; usage statistics waiting to be sent; your choice about usage statistics; and the random identifier those statistics use. The app-update check also keeps its own random identifier on your device.

This data lives in the app's own private storage. Of the twelve, Escuadra itself sends some off your device, and only while usage statistics are on: the identifier, in hashed form, with each statistic; your language with each statistic; your appearance and vibration choices when the app starts; your Study view choice when you open the Study screen; the team and level of each round you play, and your best score for that team and level when you finish it; which screens' How to play tips you closed, and whether you skipped them; roughly how many teams you have starred and how many you have played; and the statistics waiting to be sent. Deleting the app deletes all of it from your device, along with any cached photos. You can also erase it: one team's scores from that team's screen, or, in Settings under Your data, all your scores or, with Reset Escuadra, everything listed above and any cached photos, except your choice about usage statistics, the app version that last asked your phone to show a rating prompt, and the app-update check's identifier. Reset Escuadra gives usage statistics a new random identifier. Escuadra keeps no backup or sync of its own, but your device's backups (on iOS, to iCloud or a computer; on Android, to Google) may include this data. The developer has no access to this storage.

Crash reports

If you allow your device to share diagnostics, Apple (on iOS) or Google (on Android) may share anonymised crash reports with the developer. That is handled by Apple or Google and controlled in your device settings. Apart from the error message the app-update check may send after a failed start, Escuadra itself sends no crash reports.

Children

Escuadra has no account and asks for no personal information from anyone, including children. Usage statistics are anonymous and can be switched off at any time.
