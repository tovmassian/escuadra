# The site for Escuadra 2.0 — design

Supersedes `2026-09-12-gh-pages-marketing-site-design.md` for the home page and the
shared look. The design canvas, owner-approved on 2026-11-02:
https://claude.ai/artifact/95oDjvvMFE8JFmGrPWBeQC (private to the owner).

## Purpose

2.0 is when Escuadra starts being promoted: Instagram and LinkedIn send fans here,
mostly on phones, and the app's Share text links here too. The page has one job:
make a football fan curious enough to install. It does that by letting them play.

## The home page, top to bottom

1. **The opening.** Home's number wall drifting behind the headline (the app's
   `numberWall`), a lit 7 with the mark striking into its corner (the launch intro's
   shot), the app's own question as the headline ("Who wears 7?"), "Know every number."
   under it, both badges.
2. **Try one.** Three level-1 questions played as the app plays them: number,
   clues, four names, instant feedback, then the misses (`quiz.mjs`, pure and tested;
   `site.mjs` draws it). The stage shows the player's photo, framed per question
   (`focus`) and shaded only where the text sits, over the team's bands, which show
   until it loads. The facts are the app's squads, the photos its `player.photo`;
   re-check both when squads are refreshed. Ages are worked out from birth dates on
   the day.
3. **The ladder**, in the app's own level names and unlock rules.
4. **Every miss is a lesson**: Results' cards swiping by themselves, with their
   players' photos and Results' "Photo credits", and Study.
5. **From your club to your country**: 96, 157, 5, and two drifting rows of clubs
   (colour bands) and nations (flag images).
6. **A la escuadra**: the share picture, as the app draws it.
7. **Promises**: three cards, titles only (no ads, offline, five languages); the last
   call; and the footer with the listing's affiliation line and the badge credits.

The owner cut the page to these on 2026-11-03: no steps beside the teaser, no
statistics, theme or photo-credit promises, no Wikimedia line under the credits.

## Decisions

- **Player photos, and no crests.** The owner decided on 2026-11-03 to show the
  players' photos on the miss cards (#279) and on the teaser's stage. As in the app,
  they are Wikimedia's thumbnails, hotlinked and credited: Commons page, licence,
  "cropped" (guardrail 1). Each block's credits sit below it, small and muted, so on a
  phone they never come between the text and the photos.
  The teaser's credits name the team and number, not the player, and link the Commons
  page by its id, since the file name would give the answer away. Clubs are bands,
  never an emblem (guardrail 2); nations use the app's flag images
  (`assets/flags/README.md`).
- **No privacy claim on Home.** The footer's Privacy link carries it; "No personal
  data" was a 1.x claim and never comes back.
- **The Privacy link is `privacy/2/`**, and on a translated page `privacy/2/<lang>.html`.
  `docs/release.md` moves it once every live store ships 2.0, so this page goes live
  only then.
- **Fonts are the app's own and served from here** (`assets/fonts/README.md`, OFL):
  the site loads nothing from another host, except the player photos from Wikimedia.
  Body text is the visitor's system font, which saves about 1 MB of Inter.
- **The privacy pages are not regenerated.** `privacy/2/` comes from main's
  `scripts/privacy-pages.ts`; `site.css` restyles their existing class names
  (`site-header`, `brand-mark`, `theme-toggle`, `legal-page`, `language-nav`,
  `site-footer`), so a later regeneration keeps the look. Rename none of them.
- **Five languages** (#280): Home and Support in English (the root), Spanish, French,
  Italian and German (`es/`, `fr/`, `it/`, `de/`), generated by `node src/build.mjs`
  from one template (`src/home.mjs`, `src/support.mjs`, `src/layout.mjs`) and one text
  file per language (`src/text/`). Never edit a generated page; `tests/build.test.mjs`
  fails when one is out of date. Strings the app or the store listing already has come
  from `main` word for word; the headlines in `src/text/fresh.mjs` are written afresh
  per language and picked by the owner. Every language passed native review.
- **The visitor's language.** The English pages send a browser that prefers Spanish,
  French, Italian or German to its language before the first paint; a language picked
  in the header's menu wins and is remembered, so picking English stops the redirect.
  Crawlers and link previews see each language's own page (`hreflang`, canonical).
  The privacy pages, generated on main, link the English Home, which redirects.
  A visitor whose browser blocks site storage and prefers another language can't stay
  on English (the choice can't be remembered); accepted as rare.
- **Badges per language**: Apple's and Google's official artwork in each language
  (`assets/badges/README.md`), unmodified; their credit lines stay in English. Every
  file under `assets/badges/` is checked for script, event attributes and outside
  references.
- **The header on small screens.** On phones (up to 560 px) the header drops its
  Get-the-app pill (the hero's badges sit just below), and its text links collapse at
  1240 px and below (French needs the width); the footer carries the links.
- **Motion** follows the app: the wall drifts, the cards deal, the mark strikes once,
  and Reduce Motion stills all of it. The dealt cards stay opaque, set back by a dark
  layer rather than transparency, and change layer one at a time (`layer`), so no photo
  shows through or flashes over another.

## Checks

`node --test tests/*.test.mjs`: the teaser's round (`tests/quiz.test.mjs`), and every
page's links, assets, licences, badges and pinned copy (`tests/verify-site.test.mjs`),
that no generated page is out of date (`tests/build.test.mjs`), and the visitor's
language and its redirect (`tests/language.test.mjs`).
